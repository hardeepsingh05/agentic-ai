# EV Fleet Comparison: MG Windsor EV vs Tata Nexon EV

For a 100-car sales fleet in Bangalore, both are viable city EVs, but they fit slightly different priorities.

## Quick comparison

| Factor | MG Windsor EV | Tata Nexon EV |
|---|---:|---:|
| Indicative starting price | ~₹14.1 lakh ex-showroom | ~₹12.49 lakh ex-showroom |
| Battery options | 38 kWh | 30 kWh / 45 kWh |
| Claimed range | ~332 km | Up to ~489 km (45 kWh) |
| DC fast charging | ~0–80% in 45–55 min | ~10–80% in about 40–56 min |
| AC charging | ~6.5 hours on 7.4 kW wallbox | Several hours overnight |
| Boot space | 604 litres | 350 litres |
| Cabin practicality | Very strong rear room, good for city sales use | Good 5-seat usability, but more compact |
| Service network | Established MG network, but dealer performance may vary | Broad Tata service footprint and strong EV recognition |

## Fleet takeaways

- **MG Windsor EV** is better if the fleet prioritizes **cabin space and boot volume**. The 604-litre boot is a major advantage for sales reps who carry sample bags, demo material, or product cartons.
- **Tata Nexon EV** is better if the fleet prioritizes **lower entry price, wider service support, and a more proven EV brand presence** in India.
- For **Bangalore urban sales duty**, both should work well if daily routes are predictable and charging is managed centrally.
- If the fleet will often carry **bulky samples or equipment**, the **Windsor EV** has the edge.
- If the priority is **overall fleet economics and service confidence**, the **Nexon EV** is the safer default.

## Recommendation

**Recommended choice: Tata Nexon EV**

For a 100-car sales fleet, the Nexon EV is the more practical procurement choice because it starts cheaper, has broader service coverage, and benefits from Tata's stronger EV ecosystem in India. While the MG Windsor EV is far better on boot space and rear-seat room, the Nexon EV is the lower-risk fleet buy for scale deployment in Bangalore.
